How Interim Leadership Works

Grounded in Operations and Strategy

An interim executive should do more than keep the CEO's chair occupied. Cook's approach begins with listening to the Board, leadership team, employees, people receiving services and their families, and key community and government partners, to understand what is working, identify what requires immediate attention, and establish priorities that maintain organizational stability while positioning the permanent CEO for success.


As Regional Director for Dungarvin, Cook led multi-state operations across Delaware, Illinois, New Jersey, and Wisconsin, overseeing approximately 450 employees while regional revenue grew from roughly $16 million to $20 million annually. He later served as Executive Director of statewide disability provider associations in Wisconsin and Delaware, and, most recently, as Executive Director of the San Gabriel Valley Residential Facility in California. There, he maintained continuity of services through Board leadership changes, staff vacancies, regulatory challenges, and multiple concurrent organizational pressures.

An Interim Leader Who Understands the Environment

Fluent in Medicaid, Regulation, and Public Policy

Organizations serving people with disabilities operate within an increasingly complicated environment of Medicaid financing, workforce shortages, regulatory requirements, and changing service models. As Executive Director of the Ability Network of Delaware, Cook led a multi-year legislative and budget strategy that helped produce a 60 percent increase in state fiscal support for I/DD services over five years, securing $42 million in additional state appropriations and $59 million in federal matching funds.


He currently chairs the ACCSES Advisory Council and advises disability provider associations confronting Medicaid reimbursement, workforce, provider-capacity, and system-transformation challenges. That background gives him an understanding of both sides of the relationship between government and providers: the fiscal and regulatory responsibilities of public agencies, and the operational realities organizations face translating public policy into quality services.

Developing the Team, Not Replacing It

A Leadership Philosophy Built to Outlast the Assignment

Cook believes one of the most important responsibilities of an interim executive is to strengthen the organization that will remain after the interim assignment ends. His approach is straightforward: listen first, establish direction, put capable people in the right roles, give them meaningful authority, and create accountability for results. This means identifying the knowledge and talent already within an organization rather than assuming existing leadership must be replaced.

The objective is not to make an organization dependent upon its interim executive. It is to leave the organization stronger, more focused, and better prepared for its next leader.

When It Helps Most

Strategic Leadership During Periods of Change

Interim leadership can be particularly valuable when an organization is:

  • Conducting a CEO or Executive Director search following a retirement or departure
  • Managing an unexpected executive vacancy
  • Experiencing Board or senior leadership transition
  • Facing financial, regulatory, compliance, or operational challenges
  • Preparing for organizational restructuring or strategic planning
  • Managing significant changes in Medicaid reimbursement or public policy
  • Seeking to strengthen relationships with government agencies, legislators, funders, or community partners
  • Developing or strengthening its senior leadership team
  • Needing experienced executive leadership without prematurely committing to a permanent hire

The EHR Digital Health Overlay

aSuggestion for Wellness & Care is an EHR-agnostic Digital Health Overlay designed to sit seamlessly on top of existing provider systems (such as Therap, iCareManager, or others). Built with a Zero-PHI architecture, aSuggestion transforms daily routines into interactive, active treatment while capturing real-time, audit-ready compliance data, without requiring a costly, disruptive software migration.

Bridging Policy, Rates, and Practice

Addressing Industry Challenges

Potentia Policy Institute and aSuggestion join forces to bridge the gap between state Medicaid policy, rate-setting realities, and day-to-day service delivery:

DSP Retention & Labor Stabilization

Inadequate reimbursement rates and administrative overload drive historic Direct Support Professional (DSP) turnover. aSuggestion's Guided Notes, on-demand shift activities, and automated time-logging reduce administrative fatigue, keeping DSPs focused on care rather than paperwork.

Audit-Proof Revenue Protection

Rate adequacy only protects an agency if revenue is safe from clawbacks. Real-time field capture for Community-Based Outings (CBOs), Visit Trac (EVV), and automated Person-Centered Goal (PCG) cross walking ensure 100% compliance during state Medicaid audits.

Empirical Policy & Rate Modeling

Potentia's policy analysis and Brick™ Method rate studies gain a powerful field-data companion. Aggregated, de-identified engagement metrics provide policymakers and agency executives with clear evidence of actual service delivery standards, costs, and outcomes.

Scalable Technology-Enabled Care

Facing tight fiscal environments and long waitlists, combining Potentia's technical assistance with aSuggestion's remote Check-In Chats (CICt) and interactive skill tools allows agencies to expand service capacity safely.

Abuse Prevention & Ethics Certification Program

A train-the-trainer certification workshop that equips organizations serving people with intellectual and developmental disabilities to build lasting cultures of safety, ethics, and accountability.

Why It Matters

Prevention Is the Only Sustainable Strategy

Across the country, abuse and neglect in I/DD services reflect systemic weaknesses in prevention and reporting, not isolated incidents. A joint federal review found that group homes failed to report a majority of critical incidents to law enforcement or state agencies as required. A 2018 FiveThirtyEight investigation identified at least 2,400 allegations of abuse and neglect across more than 2,000 unlicensed California adult day programs, and licensed facilities have been repeatedly cited under Title 22 for personal-rights violations, supervision failures, and failure to report.


Once harm occurs, organizations face steep consequences: multi-million-dollar verdicts, insurance premium increases of 25% to 100% or more, reputational damage, and regulatory scrutiny. Prevention — not reaction — is the only strategy that protects the people served and stabilizes the organization.

Structured Training & Ethical Frameworks

Clear ethical standards, practical guidance for boundary-setting, and tools to recognize early warning signs of abuse or neglect, delivered as ongoing, scenario-based training rather than a one-time compliance exercise.

Supervision & Accountability

Guidance on staff-to-client ratios, monitoring high-risk situations such as transportation and personal care, and building regular performance and compliance reviews into daily operations.

Transparent Reporting Systems

Clear escalation pathways and a culture that encourages staff to report concerns without fear of retaliation, so warning signs surface before harm occurs.

The Certification

Abuse Prevention & Ethics Train-the-Trainer Certification Workshop

Sponsored by the Potentia Public Policy Institute, this certification workshop equips organizations with internal trainers who can sustain prevention efforts, embed ethical decision-making into daily practice, and strengthen organizational culture around safety and accountability. Participants train using Potentia's Preventing the Maltreatment of Vulnerable Individuals employee training booklet (California Edition), covering mandated reporting requirements, recognizing abuse, neglect, mistreatment, and financial abuse, and building program-level abuse prevention plans.
